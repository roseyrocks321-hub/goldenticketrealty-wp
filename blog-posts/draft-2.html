Do Tenants Have Rights When a Rental Property Sells?

Yes, and the most important thing to understand is this: in Florida, a lease survives the sale of the property. When you sell a house with tenants in place, the new owner takes it subject to the existing lease. Your tenants get to stay until the lease ends, and the buyer steps into your shoes as landlord.

A few key points under Florida law:

  • Fixed-term leases run through their end date unless the tenant agrees otherwise. A buyer who closes in March cannot remove tenants locked into a lease through December.
  • Month-to-month tenancies are more flexible. Florida requires 15 days' written notice before the end of a monthly period to terminate.
  • Security deposits must be transferred or refunded at closing, which the title company typically handles.
  • Notice of entry rules still apply to you as the seller. You must give tenants reasonable notice before showings.

This is why selling an occupied rental to a traditional retail buyer is tricky. Owner-occupant buyers want a vacant house, and they cannot get financing on a property they cannot move into on a normal timeline. Investor buyers exist, but they negotiate hard, and financing on tenant-occupied homes is harder to underwrite. That friction is exactly where a cash buyer becomes useful.

Can You Sell a Rental Property With Tenants Still Living There?

Absolutely. You do not need vacant possession to sell. There are three broad paths:

  • Wait for the lease to end, then list. Cleanest for a retail sale, but you carry the property for months with no guarantee of a fast sale afterward.
  • Negotiate a mutual exit with your tenants. Sometimes a cash-for-keys arrangement works, where you pay the tenants a negotiated amount to leave early and hand over keys in good condition.
  • Sell occupied, as-is, to a cash buyer. The tenants stay, the lease transfers, and you walk away from landlord duties on closing day.

For landlords dealing with difficult tenants, eviction in Florida is possible but slow and expensive, often taking weeks to months in Brevard County. Selling occupied skips all of that.

The 2026 Insurance Squeeze: Why Tired Landlords Are Selling

If your landlord stress has spiked in the last couple of years, there is a clear reason. Florida's property insurance market has been in crisis, and Brevard County landlords are feeling it directly. Several national carriers have pulled out of the Florida market entirely, pushing owners onto state-backed Citizens Property Insurance or thinly capitalized regional carriers that raise rates at every renewal. Some landlords we talk to have seen premiums double or triple in just a few years.

The pain does not stop at the premium notice. When insurance costs jump, mortgage servicers recalculate escrow accounts, and landlords with stable payments suddenly face hundreds of dollars per month in escrow catch-up. Add rising taxes and Brevard County's post-hurricane repair prices, and a property that cash-flowed nicely in 2021 can run negative in 2026.

None of this means every rental should be sold. But if the numbers no longer work and the property is more burden than asset, selling now, while Melbourne home values remain strong, beats holding on and hoping premiums fall. When Selling Is (and Isn't) the Right Move

Selling usually makes sense when the property consistently loses money, the tenants are more trouble than the rent is worth, you are retiring or moving away, or you want to convert equity to cash while values are healthy. It may not make sense if the property cash-flows well and your tenants are reliable long-term. There is no shame in either answer.

One more consideration: taxes. Selling a rental can trigger capital gains, and some landlords explore a 1031 exchange to defer them by reinvesting. That conversation belongs with your CPA, not with us, but we are happy to close on whatever timeline your exchange requires.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I sell my rental property in Florida if tenants have a lease?

Yes. In Florida, the lease survives the sale: the buyer takes over as landlord under the same terms until the lease ends. Many cash buyers, including us, actively purchase tenant-occupied properties with leases in place.

Do I have to evict my tenants before selling?

No. Eviction is only necessary if you want the property vacant for a specific type of buyer, and even then, Florida's eviction process can take weeks or months. Selling occupied to a cash buyer lets you skip eviction entirely.

How much notice do I have to give tenants before showings?

Florida law requires reasonable notice, and most landlords and agents use 12 to 24 hours as the standard. Your lease may specify a notice period, so check it first. Coordinating showings around tenants is one more reason occupied listings drag on.

What happens to the security deposit when I sell?

At closing, security deposits are transferred to the buyer, typically through the title company, along with any prorated rent. Your tenants' deposits stay protected and accounted for.

Will you really buy my rental with problem tenants in it?

Yes. Late payments, lease violations, unauthorized occupants, properties in rough shape: we have purchased them all. We price the property based on its real condition and situation, so you still get a fair offer without having to fix anything or remove anyone first.
