The Traditional Real Estate Route: Why It's Not Always Fast

Listing your home with a real estate agent is the conventional approach, but it's rarely the fastest. In Melbourne's market, even competitively priced homes can sit for weeks or months due to:

  • Buyer financing delays and loan approval processes
  • Required inspections, appraisals, and repair negotiations
  • Seasonal market fluctuations in Brevard County
  • Competition from new construction in West Melbourne and Viera
  • Buyer contingencies that can fall through at the last minute

According to local market data, the average time on market for Melbourne homes can range from 30 to 90 days—plus another 30-45 days for closing. If you don't have 3-5 months to spare, the traditional route may not be your best option.

Key Insight: The "list and wait" approach works for sellers with time and flexibility. If you need certainty and speed, alternative selling methods are worth exploring.

If you prefer the traditional route but need to move quickly, pricing strategy becomes critical. In Melbourne's competitive market, pricing 5-10% below comparable sales can generate multiple offers and spark bidding wars.

Option 3: For Sale By Owner (FSBO)

Selling your Melbourne home yourself eliminates agent commissions but adds significant responsibility. You'll handle marketing, showings, negotiations, and paperwork—all while trying to attract qualified buyers quickly.

FSBO works best for sellers who have real estate experience, ample free time, and don't mind negotiating directly with buyers. For most homeowners needing a fast sale, the time investment and learning curve make this option less appealing than working with professionals.

How to Choose the Right Selling Strategy

When deciding how to sell your Melbourne home, ask yourself these questions:

  • How quickly do I need to close? (Days, weeks, or months?)
  • Can I afford to make repairs or updates?
  • Am I comfortable paying 5-6% in real estate commissions?
  • Do I have the time and energy to manage showings and negotiations?
  • Is certainty of closing more important than maximum sale price?
