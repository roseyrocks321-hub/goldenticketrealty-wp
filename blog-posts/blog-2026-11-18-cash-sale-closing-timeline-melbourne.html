The Realistic Cash-Sale Timeline, Day by Day

Every home is different, but here's what a typical fast cash sale looks like in Brevard County:

  • Day 0–1: The conversation. You call us or submit your property information online. We ask a handful of questions about the home—location, condition, any known issues like roof age or code liens. This takes 10 to 15 minutes. There's no fee and no obligation at any point.
  • Day 1–2: The walkthrough. We schedule a quick walkthrough of the property, usually 20 to 30 minutes. You don't need to clean, repair, or remove anything. We're not judging your housekeeping—we're assessing the structure, systems, and what the home needs.
  • Day 2–3: The written offer. We present a firm, written cash offer, usually within 24 to 48 hours of the walkthrough. We explain exactly how we arrived at the number, based on comparable sales in your Melbourne neighborhood and the cost of the work the home needs. You can accept, decline, or think it over—pressure tactics are not how we work. Our how we buy houses page lays out this whole process.
  • Day 3–4: Contract and earnest money. If you accept, we sign a simple purchase agreement and deliver earnest money to a neutral third party—typically a licensed Florida title company or attorney's escrow account. Earnest money is your proof that we're serious: if a buyer won't put money down, that's a red flag.
  • Day 4–10: Title search and payoff work. The title company runs a title search—checking for liens, judgments, unpaid taxes, mortgages, and ownership questions—and gathers payoff statements for anything that must be cleared at closing. On a clean title, this is routine and fast.
  • Day 7–14: Closing. You sign the closing documents (the title company can often come to you), funds are wired or delivered by cashier's check, and you hand over the keys. Done.

That's the standard case: offer within 48 hours of first contact, closing within two weeks of acceptance. If your timeline is even tighter—a foreclosure sale date bearing down, a closing on your next home, a flight you can't move—tell us early. We've coordinated closings faster when title is clean and everyone moves in parallel instead of in sequence.

Can a Cash Sale Ever Close in Under a Week?

Occasionally, yes. When the seller has clear title, no mortgage or a lender that responds same-day, no HOA estoppel required, and a willingness to sign quickly, we've closed Melbourne-area sales in as little as five business days. The title search itself takes a few business days in Brevard County, and that's the true floor for most transactions—everything else is scheduling. If a buyer promises a 48-hour closing on a home with a mortgage, an HOA, or any title history at all, be skeptical. The promise isn't impossible, but it's the exception, and trustworthy buyers will tell you that upfront rather than tell you whatever closes the deal.

It's also worth knowing you can deliberately slow a cash sale down. Need 30 or 45 days to find your next place, finish a school semester, or coordinate an estate? The closing date is yours to choose. Speed is the tool—control is the point.

Why Cash Closes So Much Faster Than a Financed Sale

Why Cash Closes So Much Faster Than a Financed Sale

It's worth understanding where the speed actually comes from, because it isn't magic. In a traditional financed sale, the timeline is dominated by the lender: loan application, underwriting, appraisal, appraisal review, underwriting conditions, and finally loan documents. That alone commonly adds 30 to 45 days—and any hiccup (appraisal below contract price, insurance issues, borrower conditions) can restart the clock or kill the deal entirely.

A cash sale eliminates all of it. There's no lender, no appraisal for loan purposes, no underwriting, and no financing contingency. The only real work between contract and closing is the title search and the paperwork. That's why the whole transaction fits inside two weeks.

The Four Things That Actually Delay a Cash Closing

We promised honesty, so here it is: some cash sales don't close in two weeks. In our experience buying homes across Melbourne, Palm Bay, Cocoa, and the beachside communities, nearly every delay traces back to one of four issues.

1. Title defects. The title search may uncover liens you didn't know about—old contractor liens, code enforcement fines, unpaid HOA dues, IRS tax liens, or a prior mortgage never properly released. Clearing them takes time: paying off a lien is fast; disputing an incorrect one can take weeks.

How to get ahead of it: tell us early about anything you know of—an old judgment, a divorce decree affecting the deed, a deceased co-owner. We work with experienced Brevard County title professionals, and knowing about an issue on day one lets us start clearing it on day one instead of day ten.

2. Probate and estate issues. If the seller is an estate, Florida probate law controls who can sign and when. If the property was held in a trust, a transfer-on-death deed, or jointly with survivorship, the transfer is usually straightforward. If formal probate is needed, the court's timeline becomes part of your timeline.

How to get ahead of it: know how the property is titled before you start. If probate is involved, an experienced estate attorney is worth every dollar—and we can often structure the deal so everything else is ready to close the moment the court authorizes it. Our inherited-house guide covers this in more depth.

3. HOA estoppel letters. If the home is in one of Melbourne's many HOA communities—and much of Viera, Suntree, and West Melbourne is—the title company will order an estoppel letter confirming what you owe the association. Some HOAs turn these around quickly; others take two weeks or more.

How to get ahead of it: request the estoppel early, ideally the day the contract is signed, and follow up with the association. A small administrative step, but it sits on the critical path.

4. Mortgage and lien payoffs. Getting accurate payoff statements from lenders—especially for loans that have been sold between servicers—can take longer than expected, and FHA or VA assumptions and reverse mortgages add their own timelines.

How to get ahead of it: contact your lender for a payoff quote as soon as you decide to sell, and let us coordinate directly with them. Nothing on this list is a deal-breaker—they're just variables, and experienced buyers plan around them.

A Note on Foreclosure Timelines

If you're facing foreclosure in Melbourne, selling before the auction date is almost always better for your finances and your credit than letting it complete.
