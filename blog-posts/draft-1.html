How Does the Foreclosure Process Work in Florida?

Florida is a judicial foreclosure state, which means your lender cannot simply take the house; they have to sue you in court first. That lawsuit takes time, and that time is your window of opportunity. Here is the general sequence in Brevard County:

  • Missed payments: After roughly 90 days of missed payments, most lenders refer the file to their foreclosure attorneys.
  • Lis pendens: The lender files a lawsuit and records a lis pendens, which is public notice that the property is tied up in litigation. Your name and address now appear in the public record.
  • Summons and response window: You are served with papers. If you do nothing, the lender can move for a default judgment.
  • Judgment: The court sets the total amount owed and authorizes the sale of the property.
  • Auction: The property is scheduled for a foreclosure auction, usually at the Brevard County courthouse in Viera.

From first missed payment to auction, the process often takes six months to well over a year in Florida. But do not let that lull you. Once a judgment is entered, the auction can be scheduled quickly, and some of your best options run out of runway.

Can You Sell Your House Before the Auction?

Yes, and this is the part many homeowners do not realize. The house is still yours until the auction is complete. You can sell it, pay off the loan, and stop the foreclosure at any point before that, even after a judgment is entered. Only after the auction does ownership transfer to the highest bidder or the bank.

The key steps in a foreclosure-avoidance sale are simple:

  • Get a payoff quote from your lender so you know exactly what you owe, including back payments, fees, and attorney costs.
  • Figure out your equity. If your home is worth more than the payoff, that difference is money that belongs to you, not the bank.
  • Choose a sale method that fits the clock. A traditional listing takes months. A cash sale can close in days or weeks.
  • Close before the auction date. The title company pays off the lender directly at closing, and the foreclosure case gets dismissed.

How Does a Cash Sale Stop Foreclosure?

A cash sale stops foreclosure because the whole problem is the unpaid loan. When a cash buyer like Golden Ticket Realty purchases your house, the title company wires the payoff directly to your lender at closing. The mortgage is satisfied, the lender has no reason to continue the lawsuit, and the foreclosure is over. You get whatever equity is left, and the public record eventually reflects a paid-off loan rather than a foreclosure.

Compare that to a traditional listing. Even in a healthy Melbourne market, a financed buyer means appraisals, inspections, loan underwriting, and 45 to 60 days minimum from contract to closing. If the buyer's financing falls through at week five, you are back at square one with the auction date approaching. A cash sale has no bank approval step, no appraisal contingency, and no inspection negotiations. The offer you accept is the offer that closes.

Will You Lose Your Equity in a Foreclosure Auction?

Very possibly, yes, and this is one of the most expensive misunderstandings we see. When a home goes to auction, it typically sells for well below market value, sometimes for little more than what is owed on the loan. If you had $60,000 in equity and the house barely covers the debt at auction, that money evaporates. Even when a sale produces a surplus that technically belongs to you, tracking it down after an auction is slow, uncertain, and often requires legal help.

Selling before the auction, even at a cash-buyer price, almost always preserves far more of your equity than letting the property go to the courthouse steps. A quick sale to us is not about top dollar. It is about converting your equity into cash before the legal process destroys most of it.

What Happens to Your Credit: Selling vs. Foreclosing

Many homeowners do not realize how devastating a completed foreclosure can be beyond losing the property. It can drop your score by 100 points or more and stays on your credit report for seven years, making it harder to rent, borrow, or buy another home. A short sale also hurts, though generally less than a full foreclosure.

Selling the home and paying off the loan is categorically better. Your credit report shows the mortgage as paid and closed. Any late payments still appear, but the long shadow of a foreclosure judgment does not, and that difference matters for years.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does foreclosure take in Brevard County, Florida?

Because Florida requires lenders to go through the courts, the process typically takes six months to a year or more from first missed payment to auction. Timelines vary widely, though. If you do not respond to the lawsuit, the lender can obtain a default judgment much faster. Never assume you have more time than you do.

Can I sell my house after a foreclosure judgment but before the auction?

Yes. In Florida, you remain the owner until the auction is completed and the sale is confirmed. You can sell, pay off the full judgment amount, and stop the auction even after a judgment has been entered. This is one of the most important rights you have, and many homeowners do not learn about it until it is too late.

Will a cash buyer pay off my lender directly?

At closing, the title company wires the payoff directly to your lender from the sale proceeds. You never have to come up with the payoff money yourself, and any equity above it is paid to you.

What if I owe more than my house is worth?

If you are underwater, a standard cash sale will not fully pay off the loan. A short sale, where the lender accepts less than the full balance, may be an option. We have experience negotiating short sales and can explain how that works if it applies to you.

Does selling in foreclosure still hurt my credit?

The missed payments that led to foreclosure will show up on your credit report regardless. But selling the house and paying off the loan prevents a foreclosure judgment from being added on top, which is significantly better for your credit, your ability to rent, and your future borrowing options.
