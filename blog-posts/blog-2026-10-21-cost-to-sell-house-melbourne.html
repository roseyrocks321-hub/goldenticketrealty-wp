How Much Does It Really Cost to Sell a House in Melbourne, FL?

Published: October 21, 2026 | Golden Ticket Realty | Melbourne, FL

Most Melbourne homeowners think about selling in terms of one number: the sale price. But the price on the contract is rarely the amount that ends up in your bank account. Between agent commissions, closing costs, repairs, staging, and the monthly cost of simply owning the home while it sits on the market, a traditional sale in Melbourne can quietly consume tens of thousands of dollars before you ever see a dime. In this guide, we break down the true cost of selling a house in Melbourne, FL, line by line, so you can make a clear-eyed decision about which route is right for you.

The "Real" Number Isn't the Sale Price

We've bought houses across Melbourne, Palm Bay, Viera, and the rest of Brevard County, and one of the most common things we hear from sellers is some version of: "I had no idea it would cost that much." Sellers often fixate on the list price and mentally subtract only the commission. In reality, there are five distinct categories of cost in a traditional sale, and they add up fast.

1. Real Estate Agent Commissions (Typically 5–6%)

The largest single cost for most sellers is commission. In the Melbourne market, a full-service listing typically runs 5% to 6% of the sale price, usually split between your listing agent and the buyer's agent. On a $350,000 home—a realistic mid-range figure for many Brevard County neighborhoods—that's $17,500 to $21,000 right off the top.

Commission is the cost of professional marketing, negotiation, paperwork, and market access, and for many sellers it's money well spent. But it is not optional in a traditional sale, and it comes out of your proceeds regardless of whether you feel you got your money's worth. This is one of the biggest reasons homeowners consider alternatives like selling to a direct buyer—you can read more about how that process works on our how we buy houses page.

2. Closing Costs (Roughly 1–3%)

Even after commissions, a traditional seller in Florida usually contributes to the buyer's closing costs, prorated property taxes, title-related fees, and document stamps. A common rule of thumb is 1% to 3% of the sale price, and sellers are often surprised to learn they're expected to help with the buyer's costs in a negotiated deal. On that same $350,000 home, budget roughly $3,500 to $10,500. Florida's document stamp tax on the deed alone is a meaningful line item, and Brevard County property taxes prorated to the closing date add more.

3. Repairs, Updates, and Pre-Sale Projects

Before a Melbourne home goes on the MLS, most listing agents will recommend some combination of: fresh interior paint, landscaping, pressure washing, HVAC service, replacing worn carpet or flooring, updating light fixtures, and repairing anything flagged on a pre-listing inspection. Depending on the home's condition, this can run from a few hundred dollars to well into five figures.

And that's before the buyer's inspection. Nearly every financed purchase in Melbourne includes an inspection contingency, and buyers routinely ask for repairs or credits afterward. Roof issues are especially sensitive in Florida because of insurance requirements—a roof near the end of its life can complicate the buyer's insurance quote and kill the deal. If you want a realistic sense of what buyers will demand, our FAQs page covers what we typically see in as-is purchases.

4. Staging, Photography, and Marketing

Professional photography is nearly standard now, and staging—even partial staging—is common for homes above the entry-level price point. Budget a few hundred to a few thousand dollars depending on the level of service. These costs are smaller than commissions but come out of your pocket up front, before you know whether the home will sell.

5. Holding Costs: The Expense Nobody Budgets For

This is the cost category sellers most often overlook. Every month your home sits unsold, you're paying the mortgage (if any), homeowners insurance, property taxes, utilities, lawn care, and possibly HOA dues. In Brevard County, those carrying costs commonly add up to $2,000 to $4,000 per month or more, depending on the home.

Melbourne's market can move quickly for updated, well-priced homes—but homes that need work, or that are priced optimistically, can sit for 60, 90, or 120+ days. At $3,000 a month in holding costs, three extra months on market is $9,000 gone, on top of everything else. In Florida's heat and humidity, an unoccupied or lightly maintained home can also develop issues—mold, pests, landscaping violations from HOAs—that create new repair bills while you wait.

The Seller Concession Wildcard

Finally, many financed deals in Melbourne include seller concessions—the seller agrees to pay a portion of the buyer's closing costs, a rate buy-down, or a repair credit. Concessions are negotiated and unpredictable, but it's realistic to assume some buyers will ask, and in a slower stretch you may need to agree to keep the deal alive.

How to Run These Numbers for Your Own Home

You don't need a spreadsheet wizard to do this. Pull three numbers: your realistic sale price based on recent comparable sales in your Melbourne neighborhood (your agent or the Brevard County property appraiser's records can help), your estimated total selling costs using the ranges above, and your monthly carrying costs. Subtract the second from the first, then subtract one extra month of carrying costs for every month you realistically expect the home to take to sell. That figure is your true retail net. Now compare it against any cash offer you've received, dollar for dollar, with nothing subtracted. In our experience, sellers are often surprised at how small the gap is—and occasionally, the cash number wins outright, especially for homes that need meaningful work.
