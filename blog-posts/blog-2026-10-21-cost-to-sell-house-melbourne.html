How Much Does It Really Cost to Sell a House in Melbourne, FL?

Published: October 21, 2026 | Golden Ticket Realty | Melbourne, FL

Ask most Melbourne homeowners what it costs to sell a house, and they'll give you one number: the agent's commission. But after buying houses across Brevard County for years, we can tell you that's only the beginning. By the time you add up commissions, closing costs, repairs, staging, holding costs, and buyer concessions, the true cost of a traditional sale is far higher than most sellers expect. This guide walks through every line item—honestly—so you can see what you'd actually walk away with, and how that compares to selling your house for cash.

The Sale Price Is Not Your Number

The first thing to understand is the difference between the sale price and your net proceeds. The sale price is what goes on the contract. Your net is what lands in your bank account after everyone else gets paid. In a traditional listing, there are a lot of "everyone elses."

Real estate agents work with something called a net sheet, which estimates all the deductions from your sale price before you sign a listing agreement. Most sellers in Melbourne never see one until they're already committed. We'd rather you see the whole picture up front, because the right decision depends on it.

The True Cost of a Traditional Sale, Line by Line

Here is what a conventional listing actually costs a Melbourne seller. Commissions and fees are always negotiable, so treat these as typical ranges rather than fixed rules.

1. Agent commissions: typically 5–6% of the sale price. This is usually the biggest single cost. On a $350,000 sale—used here purely as an example—a 6% commission comes to $21,000, split between the listing side and the buyer's agent. That's real money, and it's paid even though you—not the agent—own the asset, pay the mortgage, and carry the risk while the home sits on the market.

2. Seller closing costs: roughly 1–3% of the sale price. Even with a commission-based agent, sellers in Florida typically still pay a share of closing costs: the owner's title policy, documentary stamp taxes on the deed, settlement or attorney fees, and prorated property taxes and HOA dues. On that same $350,000 example home, budget several thousand dollars more.

3. Repairs and updates: anywhere from hundreds to tens of thousands. Here's the part that blindsides people. Before listing, most Melbourne homes need at least some work to compete: fresh paint, carpet, landscaping, a roof certification, an HVAC tune-up. Then the buyer's inspection happens, and nearly every contract comes back with a repair request. In Florida's climate, roofs, air conditioners, and moisture issues are inspection magnets. We've seen "small" pre-sale punch lists balloon past $15,000, and that's before the buyer asks for credits on top.

4. Staging, photography, and cleaning: often $1,000–$3,000+. Professional photos are table stakes now. Staging, deep cleaning, pressure washing, and curb-appeal landscaping all add up, and sellers pay them out of pocket before a single dollar comes back.

5. Holding costs: quietly thousands per month. Every month your home sits unsold, you keep paying the mortgage (if any), property insurance, utilities, lawn care, and upkeep. In Melbourne's summer heat, an empty house still needs the AC running to prevent mold. If your home takes 60, 90, or 120 days to sell—which happens more often than listing agents advertise—two to four months of holding costs can easily erase thousands from your net.

6. Buyer concessions and price reductions. When a financed buyer's appraisal comes in low, or their inspection turns up issues, sellers are routinely asked to drop the price or cover credits. And if the home sits, the price reduction conversation follows. These aren't guaranteed, but they're common enough that smart sellers budget for them.

How to Decide Which Route Fits Your Situation

Neither route is "right" for everyone, and we say that plainly on every call. A traditional listing tends to make sense when your home is updated and market-ready, you're not under time pressure, and squeezing out every last dollar of retail value is worth months of process. An agent who knows Melbourne, Viera, or Palm Bay well can absolutely earn that commission in the right scenario.

A direct cash sale tends to win when one or more of these is true:

  • The home needs repairs you can't or don't want to fund
  • You're on a deadline: divorce, foreclosure auction, probate, relocation
  • The property is vacant, inherited, or tenant-occupied and draining money monthly
  • You simply value certainty and speed over maximizing every dollar
