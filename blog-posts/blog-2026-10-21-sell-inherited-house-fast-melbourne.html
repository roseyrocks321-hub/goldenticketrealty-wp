Understanding the Probate Process in Florida

Before selling an inherited property in Melbourne, it's important to understand how Florida probate works. Probate is the legal process through which a deceased person's estate is administered and distributed. In Florida, there are two main types of probate administration:

  • Formal Administration: This is the standard probate process for larger estates or when disputes exist. It requires a personal representative and typically takes six months to a year or longer.
  • Summary Administration: Available for smaller estates or when the decedent has been deceased for more than two years, this streamlined process can be completed in a matter of weeks.

During probate, the court validates the will, identifies heirs, pays creditors, and authorizes the distribution of assets. If you're named as the personal representative or executor, you have the legal authority to sell the property with court approval. Even if probate is ongoing, we can evaluate the property and prepare an offer so that everything moves forward immediately once the court gives the green light.

Common Questions About Selling an Inherited House in Melbourne

Do all heirs need to agree to sell?
In most cases, yes. All beneficiaries named in the will—or legal heirs if there is no will—must agree to the sale. If the estate is in probate, the executor or personal representative typically has the authority to sell with court approval.

What if the house has a mortgage?
If the inherited property has an outstanding mortgage, the loan balance will be paid off at closing from the sale proceeds. Any remaining equity goes to the heirs.

Can I sell before probate is complete?
This depends on the type of probate administration. In Florida, formal administration usually requires court approval for the sale, while summary administration may allow for faster disposition. We recommend consulting with a probate attorney and can refer you to trusted local professionals.

Are there tax consequences when selling an inherited house?
Inherited properties typically receive a "stepped-up basis," meaning the property's tax basis is adjusted to its fair market value at the time of the original owner's death. This can significantly reduce or eliminate capital gains taxes when you sell. However, every situation is different, and we recommend speaking with a tax professional about your specific circumstances.
