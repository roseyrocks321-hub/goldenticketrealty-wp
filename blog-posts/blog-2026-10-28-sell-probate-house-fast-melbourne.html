What Is Probate and Why Does It Complicate Selling a House?

Probate is the legal process through which a deceased person’s estate is settled. It ensures that debts are paid and that remaining assets are distributed according to the will, or under Florida intestacy laws if there is no will. In Melbourne and throughout Brevard County, probate is handled through the local circuit court. If the estate includes real estate, the court typically oversees how the property is managed and eventually sold.

This process can create serious delays for heirs who want to sell quickly. Court approval may be required before the property can be listed or sold. Creditor claims must be resolved. The property may need appraisals, and disagreements among heirs can further stall progress. All of this happens while the house sits vacant or under-maintained, accruing costs like property taxes, insurance, utilities, and lawn care.

For many families, the most practical solution is to sell the probate property to a cash buyer who understands the process and can close without the delays of traditional financing or retail buyers.

Can You Sell a House in Probate in Florida?

Yes, you can absolutely sell a house during probate in Florida, but there are important legal requirements to follow. If the estate qualifies for summary administration, which applies to smaller estates or cases where the decedent has been deceased for more than two years, the process can be relatively quick. For larger estates, formal administration is required, and the personal representative (executor) must be appointed by the court.

Once the personal representative has authority to act, they can list and sell the property. In many cases, court confirmation of the sale is not required if the personal representative’s powers are unrestricted. However, some estates do require a hearing before the sale is finalized. Working with an experienced probate attorney in Melbourne is essential to make sure every step is handled correctly.

Even with legal complexities, selling to a cash home buyer simplifies the transaction. Because there are no lender requirements, appraisal contingencies, or buyer financing delays, the sale can move forward as fast as the court process allows.

  • The heir lives far away. Managing a property from another city or state is stressful. A fast cash sale eliminates the need for travel, contractors, and long-distance coordination.
